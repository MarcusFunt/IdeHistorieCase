# Industrial workplace case research

## Chosen setting

`industrial-workplace@1.0` is set in 1913, after publication of the New York
State Factory Investigating Commission's 1912 preliminary report. The playable
role is a fictional junior investigator helping prepare a supplemental policy
memo. The case focuses on inquiry, source criticism, workplace safety, and how
recommendations might be inspected and enforced. It does not claim that the
player is a historical commissioner or that simulated proposals became law.

This case uses an investigation and policy-design interaction. Versailles
`3.1-ai` remains a negotiation. Both use the same schema-v2 importer,
initializer, context builder, turn orchestrator, proposal validator, and SQL
event/state path.

## Primary source and eligibility

- New York State Factory Investigating Commission, *Preliminary Report* (1912),
  official catalog record: <https://www.loc.gov/item/12033589/>.
- Digitized full report: <https://archive.org/details/0962976.1912.001.umich.edu>.
- The checked passages come from “Report on fire inspection work,” “Two
  Dangerous Types of Buildings.” The report's wording is “less than 150 feet in
  height” for the listed buildings whose interior finish, trim, doors, window
  frames, and sash may be ordinary wood. It also describes employees crowded
  without regard to exit facilities. They are report findings about identified
  building types, not evidence that every factory had those conditions.
- The report record establishes publication in 1912 but not a month or day.
  The corpus uses 1912-12-31 as a conservative document-date bound and only
  exposes the passages from the 1913-01-01 opening date onward.
- The source is a state commission's 1912 official report. The gameplay review
  covers only those two transcribed passages and local prototype use; no modern
  editorial text is copied. A separate archival photograph was considered but
  left pending because the New York State Archives states that rights may be
  reserved.
- The height wording was checked against the original digitized report's
  “Report on fire inspection work,” item (2):
  <https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2c/Preliminary_report_of_the_Factory_investigating_commission%2C_1912_.._%28IA_0962976.1912.001.umich.edu%29.pdf>.

## Context checks and limitations

The New York State Archives' collection overview says the commission was
created after the 1911 Triangle Waist Company fire and investigated factory
health, safety, buildings, and laws. It describes public hearings and field
investigations: <https://www.archives.nysed.gov/node/361137>. The case itself
does not state an exact death count; the 1912 report and later institutional
accounts give different counts. The role's fictional status and the
counterfactual label are explicit in the scenario bundle.

This is a primary-source review for a local educational prototype, not a
comprehensive historical or jurisdiction-wide rights review. The pending image
is not available to model context or citations.
